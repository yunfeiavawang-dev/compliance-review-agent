## 1. Purpose

This document is a human-written retrospective of KYC cases closed during the week of 2024-11-25 to 2024-11-29. It is ingested into the knowledge base like any policy document, so that learnings from past cases are retrieved with citations during future reviews. It supplements, and never overrides, the governing policies (POL-KYC-001, CDD guidelines, AML handbook, sanctions screening procedure).

Example illustration: this retrospective is the designed alternative to opaque agent memory. Each learning is written, reviewed and versioned by a person, and can be corrected or withdrawn by editing this file.

## 3. Learnings

### 3.1 Incomplete proof of address

Three individual-customer cases were submitted with a utility bill older than the 3-month limit in POL-KYC-001 section 3.1. The agent flagged the issue correctly, but two analysts initially approved with a "minor gap" note. Outcome: all three were returned to the customer for a fresh document. Guidance: an expired proof of address is a documentation failure, not a risk-rating factor, and should not be traded off against a low risk score.

### 3.2 Nominee shareholders in corporate onboarding

One corporate case showed a 22% shareholder that appeared to be a nominee holding for an undisclosed party. The beneficial-ownership threshold in the CDD guidelines is 25%, so the structure passed an automated threshold check. The analyst escalated on the nominee indicator alone. Guidance: when a shareholding sits just below the threshold and a nominee arrangement is indicated, request a beneficial-ownership declaration before applying the threshold test.

### 3.3 Sanctions screening: name transliteration

Two potential sanctions matches were false positives caused by transliteration variants of a common name. Both were cleared after comparing date of birth and nationality. Guidance: a name-only fuzzy match must not be cleared without a second identifying attribute, and the second attribute used must be recorded in the review notes.
