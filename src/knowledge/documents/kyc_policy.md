## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy establishes the requirements for customer identification, verification, and due diligence at Pacific Rim Financial Group (PRFG). It applies to all customer onboarding across retail banking, commercial banking, and payment services divisions.

All staff involved in customer onboarding must comply with this policy. Non-compliance may result in disciplinary action, regulatory sanctions, or criminal liability.

## 2. Regulatory Framework

This policy is aligned with:
- Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act (AML/CTF Act)
- Monetary Authority guidelines on customer due diligence
- Financial Action Task Force (FATF) Recommendations 10-22
- PRFG Internal Compliance Standards v5.1

## 3. Customer Identification Requirements

### 3.1 Individual Customers

The following documents are required for identity verification:

**Mandatory (at least two from different categories):**
- Category A (Photo ID): Valid passport, national identity card, or driver's license
- Category B (Proof of Address): Utility bill (within 3 months), bank statement (within 3 months), government correspondence (within 6 months)
- Category C (Tax Documentation): Tax identification number (TIN), tax return, or employer tax certificate

**Additional for non-resident customers:**
- Valid visa or work permit
- Proof of overseas address
- Source of funds declaration

### 3.2 Corporate Customers

Required documentation for corporate accounts:
- Certificate of incorporation or registration
- Memorandum and articles of association
- Board resolution authorizing account opening
- Register of directors and shareholders (beneficial owners with 25% or more ownership)
- Identification documents for all authorized signatories
- Identification documents for all beneficial owners holding 25% or more
- Latest audited financial statements (within 12 months)

### 3.3 Beneficial Ownership

Beneficial owners must be identified when:
- An individual owns or controls 25% or more of the entity
- An individual exercises significant control over the entity
- The customer is acting on behalf of another person

If beneficial ownership cannot be determined, the account opening must be escalated to the Compliance Review Committee.

## 4. Customer Risk Rating

### 4.1 Risk Factors

Each customer is assigned a risk rating based on the following factors:

| Factor | Low Risk | Medium Risk | High Risk |
|--------|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Customer Type | Salaried individual, established local business | Self-employed, new business (<2 years) | PEP, trust, shell company, NPO |
| Geography | Domestic, low-risk jurisdictions | Medium-risk jurisdictions per FATF | High-risk/sanctioned jurisdictions |
| Product/Service | Basic savings, term deposit | Business lending, trade finance | Private banking, correspondent banking |
| Transaction Pattern | Consistent with declared income | Occasional large transactions | Frequent large cash, cross-border wires |
| Source of Funds | Employment income, pension | Business revenue, investments | Inheritance, gifts, cryptocurrency |

### 4.2 Risk Scoring

- **Low Risk (Score 1-3):** Standard due diligence, review every 3 years
- **Medium Risk (Score 4-6):** Enhanced monitoring, review every 2 years
- **High Risk (Score 7-10):** Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) required, review annually, senior management approval needed

### 4.3 Automatic High-Risk Triggers

The following automatically trigger a High Risk rating:
- Customer is a Politically Exposed Person (PEP) or family member/close associate of a PEP
- Customer is from or has significant business ties to a FATF high-risk jurisdiction
- Adverse media findings related to financial crime
- Customer appears on any sanctions or watchlist
- Customer structure involves multiple layers of ownership making beneficial ownership unclear
- Cash-intensive business without adequate explanation

## 5. Due Diligence Levels

### 5.1 Standard Due Diligence (SDD)

Applied to low-risk customers:
- Verify identity using documents per Section 3
- Screen against sanctions lists and PEP databases
- Assign risk rating
- Record all verification steps and outcomes

### 5.2 Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)

Required for high-risk customers or when triggered by risk factors:
- All SDD requirements plus:
- Obtain additional identification documents
- Verify source of wealth and source of funds with supporting documentation
- Conduct enhanced background checks including adverse media screening
- Obtain senior management approval before account opening
- Document rationale for proceeding with the relationship
- Implement enhanced ongoing monitoring

### 5.3 Simplified Due Diligence

May be applied only to:
- Government entities and publicly listed companies in low-risk jurisdictions
- Requires documented risk assessment justifying simplified approach
- Must still screen against sanctions lists

## 6. Sanctions and Watchlist Screening

### 6.1 Mandatory Screening

All customers must be screened against:
- United Nations Security Council Consolidated List
- OFAC Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) List
- EU Consolidated Financial Sanctions List
- Local regulatory authority sanctions lists
- Internal watchlist maintained by PRFG Compliance

### 6.2 Screening Triggers

Screening must be performed:
- At customer onboarding
- When customer information is updated
- When new sanctions lists are published (within 24 hours)
- At periodic review dates
- When triggered by suspicious activity

### 6.3 Match Handling

- **Exact Match:** Freeze the application immediately. Escalate to Compliance Officer within 1 hour. File a suspicious activity report if confirmed.
- **Partial/Fuzzy Match:** Conduct additional verification to confirm or dismiss. Document findings. Escalate if unable to dismiss within 48 hours.
- **No Match:** Proceed with onboarding. Document screening result.

## 7. Record Keeping

- All KYC records must be retained for a minimum of 7 years after the end of the customer relationship
- Records must include: identification documents, verification results, risk assessments, screening results, review decisions, and any correspondence
- Records must be stored in the centralized compliance management system
- Access to KYC records is restricted to authorized compliance and audit staff

## 8. Ongoing Monitoring and Review

- Transaction monitoring against expected activity profile
- Periodic KYC refresh based on risk rating (Section 4.2)
- Event-driven reviews when material changes occur
- Annual certification by relationship manager that customer information is current

## 9. Escalation Procedures

| Situation | Escalate To | Timeline |
|-----------|------------|----------|
| Incomplete documentation after 2 follow-ups | Team Lead | Within 1 business day |
| Sanctions/watchlist match | Compliance Officer | Within 1 hour |
| PEP identification | Senior Compliance Manager | Within 4 hours |
| Suspicious activity during onboarding | MLRO (Money Laundering Reporting Officer) | Immediately |
| Unable to verify beneficial ownership | Compliance Review Committee | Within 2 business days |
| Customer refuses to provide required documents | Decline and report to Team Lead | Immediately |
