## 1. Overview

MAS Notice 626 (issued under Section 27B of the Monetary Authority of Singapore Act) sets out requirements for banks in Singapore to implement adequate policies, procedures, and controls to detect and deter money laundering (ML) and terrorism financing (TF). This document contains extracts of key requirements relevant to KYC onboarding and ongoing monitoring.

All bank staff must treat this as a regulatory minimum. Internal policies (POL-KYC-001, CDD Guidelines) may impose stricter requirements.

## 2. Customer Due Diligence (CDD) Requirements

### 2.1 When CDD Must Be Performed

A bank shall perform CDD measures when:
- Establishing business relations with any customer
- Carrying out any transaction exceeding S$20,000 for a customer with whom the bank has no established business relationship
- There is suspicion of ML/TF, regardless of transaction amount
- The bank has doubts about the veracity of previously obtained CDD information

### 2.2 Identification and Verification

For natural persons, the bank shall obtain at minimum:
- Full name, including aliases
- Unique identification number (NRIC, passport, or equivalent)
- Date of birth
- Nationality
- Residential address

Verification must be performed using reliable, independent source documents. For Singapore residents: NRIC or valid passport. For foreign nationals: valid passport with entry stamp or valid pass issued by ICA.

### 2.3 Beneficial Ownership

For legal persons and arrangements, the bank shall identify and take reasonable measures to verify the identity of beneficial owners. A beneficial owner is any natural person who:
- Owns more than 25% of shares or voting rights
- Exercises control through other means (e.g., shareholder agreements, power to appoint directors)
- Is the settlor, trustee, protector, or beneficiary of a trust

If the bank is unable to identify the beneficial owner, it shall not establish the business relationship.

## 3. Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)

### 3.1 When EDD Is Required

EDD is mandatory when the bank assesses that there is higher ML/TF risk, including:
- Customer or beneficial owner is a PEP (domestic or foreign)
- Business relationship with persons from or in countries with inadequate AML/CFT measures (as identified by FATF)
- Complex or unusually large transactions with no apparent economic or lawful purpose
- Private banking relationships
- Non-face-to-face business relationships where safeguards are not equivalent to face-to-face
- Correspondent banking relationships

### 3.2 EDD Measures

Enhanced measures shall include:
- Obtaining senior management approval before establishing or continuing the business relationship
- Taking reasonable measures to establish the source of wealth and source of funds
- Conducting enhanced ongoing monitoring of the business relationship
- Obtaining additional identification information and verifying it using additional reliable sources

### 3.3 PEP Requirements

For PEP customers, in addition to standard EDD, the bank shall:
- Have appropriate risk management systems to determine whether a customer or beneficial owner is a PEP
- Obtain senior management approval before establishing (or continuing, for existing) business relationships
- Take reasonable measures to establish source of wealth and source of funds
- Conduct enhanced ongoing monitoring

A domestic PEP who has left office may be downgraded from PEP status after a risk assessment, taking into account the level of influence the person continues to hold.

## 4. Ongoing Monitoring

### 4.1 Transaction Monitoring

Banks shall monitor transactions to ensure they are consistent with the bank's knowledge of the customer, the customer's business and risk profile, and where necessary, the source of funds. This includes:
- Identifying transactions that are complex, unusually large, or have unusual patterns with no apparent economic or lawful purpose
- Monitoring for transactions involving high-risk countries
- Reviewing and keeping up-to-date customer information

### 4.2 Review Trigger Events

CDD information must be reviewed and updated when:
- A transaction of significance occurs
- A change occurs in the way the account is operated
- The bank's documentation standards change
- The bank becomes aware that it lacks sufficient information about an existing customer

## 5. Record Keeping

### 5.1 Retention Requirements

Banks shall maintain records of:
- All CDD information obtained, including identification and verification documents — for at least 5 years after termination of the business relationship
- All transaction records — for at least 5 years from the date of the transaction
- All internal and external reports related to ML/TF — for at least 5 years from the date of the report

### 5.2 Format

Records must be sufficient to permit reconstruction of individual transactions to provide evidence for prosecution of criminal activity. They must be readily available to MAS and other competent authorities.

## 6. Suspicious Transaction Reporting (STR)

### 6.1 Obligation to File

If a bank has reasonable grounds to suspect that any transaction or attempted transaction is related to ML/TF, the bank shall file an STR with the Suspicious Transaction Reporting Office (STRO) as soon as reasonably practicable, regardless of the amount involved.

### 6.2 Tipping Off

No bank or officer shall disclose to any person that an STR has been filed or is being filed, or any information that may reveal such filing.

## 7. Sanctions Compliance

### 7.1 Screening Obligations

Banks shall screen all customers, beneficial owners, and connected parties against:
- UN Security Council consolidated list
- Lists under MAS Regulations (Terrorism Financing, DPRK, Iran)
- OFAC SDN list (for USD transactions)
- EU consolidated list (for EUR transactions)
- Other applicable lists as directed by MAS

### 7.2 Match Handling

Where a potential match is identified, the bank shall:
- Immediately escalate to the compliance function
- Not proceed with the transaction or business relationship until the match is resolved
- If a true match is confirmed, freeze the assets and report to MAS immediately
- Document the entire match resolution process for audit purposes
