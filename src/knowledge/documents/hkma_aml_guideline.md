## 1. Overview

The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) issues guidelines on AML/CFT under Section 7 of the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (AMLO, Cap. 615). These guidelines apply to all authorized institutions (AIs) operating in Hong Kong.

This document summarizes the key requirements relevant to KYC onboarding, risk assessment, and ongoing due diligence at Pacific Rim Financial Group's Hong Kong operations.

## 2. Risk-Based Approach (RBA)

### 2.1 Institutional Risk Assessment

AIs must conduct regular institutional risk assessments that consider:
- Customers: types of customers, geographic locations, industries
- Products and services: vulnerability to ML/TF
- Delivery channels: face-to-face vs. non-face-to-face
- Geographic factors: countries and regions of operation

### 2.2 Customer Risk Assessment

AIs shall assess ML/TF risk for each customer at onboarding and on an ongoing basis. Risk factors include:
- Customer type (individual, corporate, trust, foundation)
- Business or professional activity
- Country/geographic risk
- Product/service risk
- Delivery channel risk
- Other indicators (PEP status, adverse media, complex structures)

### 2.3 Risk Categories

AIs shall categorize customers into at least three risk levels:
- **Low risk**: Customers with low inherent risk and no adverse indicators
- **Medium risk**: Customers with moderate risk factors manageable through standard measures
- **High risk**: Customers requiring enhanced scrutiny — PEPs, high-risk jurisdictions, complex structures, adverse media, unusually large or atypical transactions

## 3. Customer Due Diligence

### 3.1 Standard CDD

Standard CDD for natural persons requires:
- Full name and any aliases used
- Date of birth
- Nationality and residency status
- Unique identification number and document type
- Residential address (verified by utility bill, bank statement, or government correspondence dated within 3 months)
- Purpose and intended nature of the business relationship
- Expected pattern of transactions

For legal persons, additional requirements include:
- Full name and registration number
- Registered office address and principal place of business
- Constitutional documents (certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association)
- Board resolution authorizing account opening
- Identity of directors, authorized signatories, and persons with significant control

### 3.2 Simplified CDD

Simplified measures may be applied only where the AI has assessed that the ML/TF risk is low, and only for specified categories such as:
- Listed companies and their subsidiaries
- Financial institutions regulated in equivalent jurisdictions
- Government bodies in Hong Kong

Simplified CDD is not permitted where there is any suspicion of ML/TF.

### 3.3 Enhanced CDD

Enhanced CDD is mandatory for:
- PEPs (foreign, domestic, and international organization PEPs) and their family members and close associates
- Customers connected to higher-risk countries or jurisdictions
- Correspondent banking relationships
- Private banking or wealth management clients
- Complex or unusual transactions
- Non-face-to-face relationships without equivalent safeguards

## 4. Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs)

### 4.1 PEP Definition

A PEP is an individual who is or has been entrusted with a prominent public function, including:
- Heads of state, heads of government, ministers
- Members of parliament or legislative bodies
- Senior government officials, judiciary, or military
- Senior executives of state-owned enterprises
- Senior officials of political parties

Family members (spouse, children, parents, siblings) and close associates are also covered.

### 4.2 PEP Identification

AIs shall implement risk management systems to determine whether a customer or beneficial owner is a PEP. This includes:
- Screening against PEP databases at onboarding and periodically
- Self-declaration by the customer
- Adverse media screening that may reveal PEP status

### 4.3 PEP De-classification

A former PEP may be de-classified after the AI assesses:
- The period elapsed since leaving office (minimum 12 months; in practice 2+ years recommended)
- Whether the person retains informal influence
- The level of corruption risk in the person's country
- Whether the person continues to hold positions that may confer political influence

The decision to de-classify must be documented and approved by senior management.

## 5. Source of Wealth and Source of Funds

### 5.1 Definitions

- **Source of Wealth (SoW)**: The origin of the customer's entire body of wealth — how they accumulated their total assets (e.g., inheritance, business ownership, employment income, investments)
- **Source of Funds (SoF)**: The origin of the particular funds involved in the business relationship or transaction — where the money for a specific deposit or transaction comes from

### 5.2 Verification

For high-risk customers, SoW and SoF must be verified through:
- Independent, reliable documentation (audited financial statements, tax returns, property valuations, employment contracts)
- Cross-referencing with public records and business registries
- Obtaining information from independent data sources

A self-declaration alone is not sufficient for high-risk customers. At least one independent corroborating document is required.

### 5.3 Red Flags

The following are indicators of potentially suspicious SoW/SoF:
- SoW inconsistent with the customer's known profile or occupation
- Inability or unwillingness to provide supporting documentation
- Funds from jurisdictions with no apparent connection to the customer
- Sudden unexplained changes in financial profile
- Complex or layered transactions designed to obscure the funds' origin

## 6. Document Verification Standards

### 6.1 Acceptable Documents

Category A (Identity):
- Passport (valid, machine-readable)
- Hong Kong Identity Card (HKID)
- National identity card from home jurisdiction

Category B (Address):
- Utility bill (within 3 months)
- Bank statement (within 3 months)
- Government correspondence (within 3 months)
- Tenancy agreement (current)

### 6.2 Document Integrity

AIs must assess document authenticity. Red flags for potentially fraudulent documents:
- Inconsistencies between MRZ data and visual inspection zone
- Unusual fonts, spacing, or alignment
- Signs of tampering, erasure, or overwriting
- Lamination defects or irregularities
- Mismatch between document quality and issuing country's standards
- Information inconsistencies across multiple submitted documents

Where document fraud is suspected, the AI must decline the business relationship and consider filing an STR.

## 7. Ongoing Monitoring

### 7.1 Transaction Monitoring

AIs shall implement systems to identify transactions that:
- Are inconsistent with the customer's known profile
- Are unusually complex or large without clear economic rationale
- Involve high-risk jurisdictions
- Involve rapid movement of funds (layering)
- Show patterns associated with known ML/TF typologies

### 7.2 Periodic Review

Customer risk profiles and CDD information must be reviewed:
- High-risk customers: annually (or more frequently if warranted)
- Medium-risk customers: every 2-3 years
- Low-risk customers: every 5 years

Trigger events requiring immediate review: change in customer behavior, adverse media, change in beneficial ownership, significant changes in transaction patterns.
