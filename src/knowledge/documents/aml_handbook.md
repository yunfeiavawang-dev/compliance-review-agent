## 1. Overview

This handbook provides practical guidance for identifying, preventing, and reporting money laundering and terrorism financing at Pacific Rim Financial Group (PRFG). All employees must complete annual AML training and are required to report suspicious activities.

## 2. What is Money Laundering?

Money laundering is the process of making illegally obtained money appear legitimate. It typically occurs in three stages:

1. **Placement:** Introducing illicit funds into the financial system (e.g., cash deposits, purchasing monetary instruments)
2. **Layering:** Concealing the trail through complex transactions (e.g., wire transfers between accounts, shell companies, trade-based laundering)
3. **Integration:** Reintroducing funds into the legitimate economy (e.g., real estate purchases, business investments)

## 3. Red Flags and Suspicious Indicators

### 3.1 Customer Behavior Red Flags

- Reluctance to provide identification or business documentation
- Providing false, inconsistent, or suspicious identification documents
- Unusual concern about compliance reporting requirements
- Attempting to influence staff to avoid reporting obligations
- Using intermediaries or nominees without clear business rationale
- Frequent changes in account signatories or authorized persons

### 3.2 Transaction Red Flags

- Cash transactions just below reporting thresholds (structuring/smurfing)
- Large cash deposits inconsistent with the customer's business profile
- Rapid movement of funds between accounts with no apparent business purpose
- Wire transfers to or from high-risk jurisdictions without clear explanation
- Round-dollar transactions with no apparent business logic
- Frequent transactions between seemingly unrelated parties
- Unusual patterns in trade finance (over/under invoicing, mismatched goods descriptions)

### 3.3 Account Activity Red Flags

- Dormant account suddenly receiving large deposits
- Account used primarily as a pass-through (funds in and out quickly with no retention)
- Multiple accounts opened in a short period with similar transaction patterns
- Accounts held in names of nominees or shell companies with no clear business purpose
- Significant unexplained changes in account activity patterns

### 3.4 High-Risk Business Types

The following business categories require enhanced scrutiny:
- Money service businesses and foreign exchange dealers
- Cash-intensive businesses (restaurants, retail, parking, car washes)
- Real estate agencies and developers
- Dealers in precious metals and stones
- Non-profit organizations with international operations
- Virtual asset service providers (cryptocurrency exchanges)
- Professional service firms (lawyers, accountants) holding client funds

## 4. Suspicious Activity Reporting

### 4.1 When to Report

You MUST file a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) when you:
- Know or suspect a transaction involves proceeds of crime
- Know or suspect a transaction is related to terrorism financing
- Have reasonable grounds to suspect money laundering based on the red flags above
- Are instructed by law enforcement to monitor an account

**Important:** You do NOT need to prove that money laundering has occurred. A reasonable suspicion is sufficient and required to trigger a report.

### 4.2 How to Report

1. Do NOT alert the customer (tipping off is a criminal offense)
2. Document your observations and the basis for your suspicion
3. Submit an internal SAR through the compliance management system within 24 hours
4. The MLRO will review and determine whether to file an external report with the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU)
5. Continue normal business interaction unless instructed otherwise by the MLRO

### 4.3 Tipping Off

It is a criminal offense to disclose to any person that:
- A SAR has been filed or is being considered
- A money laundering investigation is being conducted
- Information has been shared with law enforcement

Penalties include imprisonment and substantial fines.

## 5. Customer Due Diligence for AML

### 5.1 Source of Funds vs. Source of Wealth

- **Source of Funds (SOF):** The origin of the specific funds involved in the transaction or account. Example: salary payment, property sale proceeds, inheritance.
- **Source of Wealth (SOW):** How the customer accumulated their total net worth over time. Example: employment history, business ownership, investments.

Both must be verified for high-risk customers. SOF must be verified for all customers when the transaction amount exceeds thresholds or appears unusual.

### 5.2 Transaction Monitoring

PRFG employs automated and manual monitoring:
- Automated alerts for transactions exceeding thresholds
- Pattern detection for structuring and pass-through behavior
- Peer group analysis comparing activity against similar customer profiles
- Manual review of flagged transactions by compliance analysts

### 5.3 Cash Transaction Thresholds

| Transaction Type | Reporting Threshold | Action |
|-----------------|---------------------|--------|
| Single cash deposit/withdrawal | ≥ $10,000 (or equivalent) | Automatic Currency Transaction Report (CTR) |
| Aggregate same-day cash | ≥ $10,000 (or equivalent) | Automatic CTR |
| Wire transfer | ≥ $15,000 (or equivalent) | Enhanced record keeping |
| Cash purchase of monetary instruments | ≥ $3,000 (or equivalent) | Record customer identity |

## 6. Sanctions Compliance

### 6.1 Prohibited Activities

PRFG employees must NOT:
- Process transactions involving sanctioned individuals, entities, or countries
- Provide financial services to designated persons
- Facilitate the circumvention of sanctions
- Handle funds or assets belonging to designated persons

### 6.2 Sanctions Screening Requirements

All new customers, counterparties, and transactions must be screened against applicable sanctions lists. Refer to KYC Policy Section 6 for detailed screening procedures.

## 7. Record Keeping for AML

- All transaction records: minimum 7 years
- SARs and related documentation: minimum 7 years from filing date
- Customer identification records: minimum 7 years after end of relationship
- Training records: minimum 5 years
- All records must be accessible within 48 hours of a regulatory request

## 8. Penalties for Non-Compliance

### 8.1 Regulatory Penalties

- Fines up to $10 million or 10% of annual turnover
- Revocation of banking license
- Restrictions on business activities
- Public censure

### 8.2 Individual Penalties

- Criminal prosecution for failure to report or tipping off
- Imprisonment of up to 7 years
- Personal fines
- Professional disqualification
- Termination of employment

## 9. Annual Training Requirements

All employees must complete:
- Basic AML awareness training (all staff, annual)
- Enhanced AML training (customer-facing staff, annual)
- Specialized compliance training (compliance team, quarterly)
- Ad-hoc training when regulations or policies change

Training completion is tracked by HR and Compliance. Failure to complete mandatory training within the deadline will be reported to the employee's line manager and may affect performance evaluations.
